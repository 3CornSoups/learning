Abstract
Hypergraph-based methods extend structured
retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) by or-
ganizing external knowledge as multi-entity
relational structures, helping large language
models (LLMs) generate better-grounded an-
swers. However, existing structured RAG
systems typically assume static corpus con-
struction, requiring the retrieval structure to
be rebuilt over the expanded corpus when
new documents arrive. To address this lim-
itation, we introduce HyperRAG-IHM, a
hypergraph-based RAG framework that per-
forms incremental hyperedge maintenance to
incorporate newly arriving knowledge with-
out reconstructing the historical retrieval struc-
ture. HyperRAG-IHM links relational evi-
dence extracted from new documents to ex-
isting hypergraph knowledge, avoiding recon-
struction of the entire retrieval structure. It
uses LSH to localize candidate historical hy-
peredges, then applies multi-dimensional com-
parison and a three-action decision policy to in-
tegrate new relational evidence. Experiments
show that HyperRAG-IHM achieves answer
quality comparable to or better than full recon-
struction under 5D scoring and 8D pairwise
evaluation, and remains robust across ablation,
structural behavior, cross-dataset, and cross-
answer-model analyses.
1 Introduction
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) serves as a
core paradigm for knowledge-intensive NLP tasks
by enabling large language models (LLMs) to
generate responses grounded in external knowl-
edge rather than relying solely on parametric mem-
ory ( Lewis et al. , 2020; Guu et al. , 2020; Izac-
ard and Grave , 2021; Borgeaud et al. , 2022; Gao
et al. , 2023). Such external grounding is es-
pecially important for reducing hallucination in
knowledge-intensive generation ( Ji et al. , 2023).
*Corresponding authors.
Early RAG systems relied on dense passage re-
trieval ( Karpukhin et al. , 2020). Recent work
has moved toward structured retrieval methods, in-
cluding graph-based, hierarchical, and memory-
inspired approaches ( Pan et al. , 2024; Edge et al. ,
2024; Guo et al. , 2025; Sarthi et al. , 2024; Gutiér-
rez et al. , 2024; Peng et al. , 2025), which explic-
itly capture relational knowledge beyond ﬂat text
chunks. Hypergraph-based RAG further extends
this direction by representing multi-entity associa-
tions as hyperedges ( Feng et al., 2026, 2019).
In real-world deployments, domain knowledge
does not remain ﬁxed, and retrieval systems often
need to operate over collections where new infor-
mation arrives over time ( Hou et al. , 2025). Fresh
clinical case reports, newly published biomedical
literature, revised guidelines, and other domain-
speciﬁc documents continuously change the evi-
dence available to the system. However, these
structured RAG systems are still largely built
around static corpus construction ( Zhang et al. ,
2025b). How to update the retrieval structure
while preserving its relational organization re-
mains underexplored when new documents arrive.
A retrieval system that cannot absorb these up-
dates gradually loses coverage, while incorporat-
ing new evidence without explicit maintenance
can introduce duplicate or conﬂicting relational
evidence into the retrieval structure. We refer to
this requirement as corpus expansion: an existing
knowledge base must absorb newly arrived doc-
uments over time while keeping its structured re-
trieval representation reliable.
For structured RAG systems, corpus expansion
creates a tension between update cost and repre-
sentation reliability. A recent preprint on tempo-
ral RAG notes that static-corpus assumptions leave
update costs and retrieval stability insufﬁciently
examined ( Han et al. , 2025). A recent preprint,
EraRAG, builds a uniﬁed retrieval index over the
expanded corpus, but its full-reconstruction setting

repeats extraction and construction over historical
documents, causing update cost to grow with accu-
mulated corpus size ( Zhang et al. , 2025b). Avoid-
ing full reconstruction still requires deciding how
newly extracted relational evidence should con-
nect to the existing retrieval structure. Without
explicit maintenance, these new-old relationships
remain implicit, making it difﬁcult to control rela-
tional redundancy and preserve coherent retrieved
context as updates accumulate. The central chal-
lenge is therefore:
Can we build a RAG system whose
knowledge structure can be incremen-
tally updated as new knowledge arrives,
while retaining strong organization and
retrieval quality?
We develop HyperRAG-IHM, an incre-
mental hyperedge maintenance framework for
hypergraph-based RAG. HyperRAG-IHM treats
relational evidence extracted from newly arrived
documents as maintenance targets and links it
to existing hypergraph knowledge. It ﬁrst uses
an auxiliary LSH index to localize candidate
historical hyperedges, and then compares new
and existing relation records with entity, seman-
tic, keyword, source, support, and speciﬁcity
signals. The resulting maintenance decisions
update the hyperedge layer through aggregation,
replacement, or separate retention, allowing the
retrieval structure to absorb new knowledge while
controlling relational redundancy. We instantiate
HyperRAG-IHM on HyperRAG, whose hyper-
edges provide concrete relation-level records
containing entity sets, descriptions, keywords,
source identiﬁers, and weights ( Feng et al. , 2026,
2019).
Our contributions are:
• We propose HyperRAG-IHM, an incremen-
tal maintenance framework that combines
LSH-based candidate localization, multi-
dimensional hyperedge comparison, and a
three-action maintenance policy.
• We identify corpus expansion in hypergraph-
based RAG as an incremental hyperedge
maintenance problem, with the goal of incor-
porating newly arrived knowledge without re-
constructing the existing retrieval structure.
• Experiments covering quality, efﬁciency,
structural behavior, ablation, cross-dataset
generalization, and cross-model robustness
show that HyperRAG-IHM substantially re-
duces update cost while preserving competi-
tive full-reconstruction answer quality.
2 Related Work
2.1 Structured Retrieval-Augmented
Generation
Early RAG paired dense passage retrieval with
generation ( Karpukhin et al. , 2020; Lewis et al. ,
2020; Guu et al. , 2020; Izacard and Grave ,
2021). Benchmarks such as KILT further consoli-
dated knowledge-intensive language tasks around
shared evidence sources ( Petroni et al. , 2021). Re-
cent systems increasingly use corpus structure to
support relational and multi-hop reasoning ( Peng
et al. , 2025). GraphRAG builds entity relation
graphs and community reports ( Edge et al. , 2024;
Traag et al., 2019), LightRAG combines graph and
vector retrieval ( Guo et al. , 2025), RAPTOR con-
structs hierarchical summary trees ( Sarthi et al. ,
2024), and HippoRAG coordinates LLMs, knowl-
edge graphs, and graph search ( Gutiérrez et al. ,
2024). HyperRAG further uses hypergraphs to
model multiway relations and thematic associa-
tions ( Feng et al. , 2026, 2019). These methods
motivate structured retrieval, but they mainly orga-
nize retrieval over a ﬁxed corpus rather than main-
taining hypergraph structure during corpus expan-
sion.
2.2 Dynamic and Continual RAG
Self-RAG and a CRAG preprint study inference-
time retrieval control, critique, and correction
during generation ( Asai et al. , 2024; Y an et al. ,
2024). Beyond retrieval-speciﬁc methods, re-
cent work studies reasoning efﬁciency and relia-
bility through selective chain-of-thought compres-
sion and adaptive reﬁnement (Zhang et al., 2026c).
Recent preprints further explore adaptive reﬁne-
ment and late-stage error mitigation ( Zhang et al. ,
2026a, 2025a), as well as explicit or coarse-to-
ﬁne reasoning over multimodal and tabular in-
puts ( Zhang et al. , 2026b; Huang et al. , 2026).
These approaches optimize inference-time com-
putation or intermediate reasoning representations
without maintaining the underlying retrieval struc-
ture; when the corpus itself expands, index-level
maintenance remains a separate problem. Con-
tinual neural information retrieval studies retrieval
systems that adapt over time, including deﬁnitions,

datasets, and evaluation protocols for lifelong set-
tings ( Hou et al. , 2025). A recent temporal-RAG
preprint also highlights update-cost and retrieval-
stability concerns under evolving knowledge (Han
et al. , 2025). The EraRAG preprint studies cor-
pus expansion at the index-construction level and
uses hyperplane-based LSH to localize insertions
in graph-based retrieval indexes ( Zhang et al. ,
2025b). However, none of these methods ad-
dresses maintenance of structured hyperedge rep-
resentations under corpus expansion, where new
evidence must be reconciled with existing hyper-

2.3 Knowledge Maintenance, Entity
Resolution, and LSH
Knowledge graph research studies dynamic up-
dates through entity alignment and temporal link
prediction ( Sun et al. , 2020; Y an et al., 2021; Jin
et al., 2020), but these methods usually operate on
binary graph edges rather than hyperedges among
multiple entities. Entity resolution and record
linkage use candidate blocking followed by multi-
feature comparison ( Christophides et al. , 2020;
Papadakis et al. , 2020), providing a useful anal-
ogy for restricting and comparing candidate hyper-
edges. LSH supports efﬁcient approximate search,
near duplicate detection, and streaming data pro-
cessing ( Indyk and Motwani , 1998; Manku et al. ,
2007; Leskovec et al. , 2020); SimHash and Min-
Hash provide widely used similarity estimators
(Charikar, 2002; Broder, 1997). In this paper,
these ideas serve as background for candidate lo-
calization; the concrete maintenance mechanism
is deﬁned in Section 4.1.
3 Preliminaries and Problem
Formulation
3.1 Hypergraph Representation and
Hyperedge Types
HyperRAG (Feng et al., 2026) organizes extracted
knowledge as a hypergraph H = ( V, E), where V
denotes entity vertices and E denotes hyperedges
over these vertices. In the hypergraph-based repre-
sentation used in this work, the LLM extraction
process produces entities, low-order hyperedges,
and high-order hyperedges in the same extraction
pass. Each hyperedge is deﬁned by an entity set
and is associated with a textual description, key-
words, source identiﬁers, and an edge weight, so
that it can encode a relation or association among
a group of entities.
Low-order hyperedges capture direct entity-
level relational evidence: when a small number of
entities are explicitly related in a text chunk, the
system extracts a low-order hyperedge recording
the relational description, keywords, and strength.
High-order hyperedges instead organize broader
thematic associations among multiple related en-
tities identiﬁed during the same extraction pass.
During incremental updates, high-order hyper-
edges follow the inherited key-based aggregation
path: records with the same normalized entity set
are consolidated, while records with different en-
tity sets remain separate. HyperRAG-IHM applies
its LSH-based three-action maintenance speciﬁ-
cally to low-order hyperedges, where newly ex-
tracted relational evidence is most likely to overlap
with or reﬁne existing records.
3.2 Incremental Hyperedge Maintenance
Problem
Let ∆Dt denote the batch of new texts arriving at
update step t. Before this update, the system main-
tains an existing HyperRAG index built from the
previously observed corpus. Its graph component
is denoted as Ht−1 = ( Vt−1, Et−1), where Vt−1
contains extracted entity vertices and Et−1 con-
tains the stored hyperedges. The system also main-
tains an auxiliary LSH maintenance state St−1 for
active low-order hyperedges, including their nor-
malized records and indexing metadata used for
local candidate retrieval.
Given ∆Dt, the update objective is to construct
an expanded hypergraph Ht and a synchronized
auxiliary state St without rebuilding the historical
hypergraph from scratch, such that:
1. Ht incorporates the knowledge extracted
from the expanded corpus;
2. redundant, overlapping, or superseded low-
order hyperedges are maintained through ag-
gregation, replacement, or separate retention
rather than being accumulated without con-
trol;
3. extraction and maintenance are restricted to
∆Dt and the hyperedges extracted from it,
without re-running extraction over the histor-
ical corpus.

4 HyperRAG-IHM
For each newly extracted low-order hyperedge,
HyperRAG-IHM localizes candidate historical
edges, compares them with interpretable relational
signals, and applies one of three maintenance ac-
tions. The resulting update is committed jointly to
the main hypergraph and the auxiliary LSH state,
so that subsequent updates operate on a synchro-
nized structure. Figure 1 illustrates the overall
workﬂow.
4.1 LSH-Based Candidate Retrieval
Motivated by EraRAG’s use of LSH-based local-
ization in corpus expansion ( Zhang et al. , 2025b),
HyperRAG-IHM maintains an auxiliary LSH in-
dex that maps semantically similar edge repre-
sentations to the same or nearby buckets ( Indyk
and Motwani, 1998), avoiding exhaustive compar-
ison between each new edge and all historical
edges. For each new low-order hyperedge, the
index returns a bounded candidate set including
matches under the canonical entity-set key κ(e),
nearby LSH buckets, and entity-overlap candi-
dates; merge and replacement decisions are made
by the comparison and decision modules below.
The index reuses a ﬁxed random hyperplane
matrix P ∈ Rk×d across incremental stages and
maintains bucket labels for active low-order hyper-
edges. This sidecar state provides stable locality
preserving access to historical edges while leaving
the main hypergraph as the authoritative retrieval
structure.

hyperplane similarity estimation ( Charikar, 2002),
each low-order hyperedge is projected into a bi-

Candidate Retrieval. For a new edge enew, can-

and the ﬁnal candidate set is capped by a ﬁxed
maximum size. The retrieved candidates are then
ranked by the preﬁlter score in Section 4.2.
4.2 Multi-Dimensional Edge Comparison
After candidate retrieval, each candidate pair
(enew, eold) is represented by a set of inter-
pretable comparison features. This design fol-
lows the broad entity-resolution pattern of block-
ing/ﬁltering followed by multi-feature comparison
(Christophides et al., 2020; Papadakis et al., 2020).
Conceptually, these deterministic signals cover
three aspects of a maintenance decision: identity
and overlap, semantic compatibility, and evidence
strength.
Entity, keyword, and source overlaps are com-
puted with set-similarity measures such as Jaccard
similarity. Containment and overlap-coefﬁcient
signals are included because two related hyper-
edges may not have identical entity sets but can
still describe nested or partially overlapping rela-
tions (Broder, 1997). Semantic similarity uses the
same textual edge representation as LSH signa-
tures, allowing relation meaning to be compared
beyond exact keyword or entity overlap. The re-
sulting signals include entity overlap, overlap co-
efﬁcient, keyword overlap, source overlap, source
gain, semantic similarity, and containment. Sup-
port qsup(e) is derived from source count and edge

4.3 Three-Action Decision Mechanism
Given the top-ranked candidate e∗
old, the system
applies a deterministic decision tree based on the
comparison features deﬁned in Section 4.2. Here,
Snew and Sold denote the entity sets of enew and
e∗
old, respectively. The three actions cover compat-
ible, superseding, and distinct relations between
new and existing hyperedges. The tree uses entity
and semantic similarity, merge and replacement
scores, and support/speciﬁcity deltas. If no can-
didate exists, the new edge is kept. When the top
candidate shares the same sorted entity set, the sys-
tem ﬁrst checks replacement; if replacement is not
triggered, the payloads are merged as repeated ev-
idence for the same relation. When the top can-
didate has a contained or highly compatible entity
set, the system can also merge the related evidence
according to semantic and merge scores. If merge
conditions are not met, the system checks replace-
ment and then defaults to keeping both when simi-
larity or evidence strength is insufﬁcient.
Merge (aggregate compatible evidence).
Merge aggregates compatible evidence into an
existing maintenance unit. It applies to repeated
evidence over the same sorted entity set and to
contained or highly compatible candidates such
as Snew ⊂ Sold. The decision uses semantic and
merge scores together with supporting cues such
as keyword overlap, source overlap, or no speci-
ﬁcity increase. It deduplicates and concatenates
descriptions, unions keyword and source sets, and
sums weights.
Replace_old (new evidence supersedes old).
Replacement is triggered when strong entity and
semantic agreement is paired with a support or
speciﬁcity advantage, or when reverse contain-
ment Sold ⊂ Snew indicates a more complete and
better supported relation. If the canonical keys dif-
fer, the old edge is marked inactive and the new
edge is inserted; if they match, the maintenance
unit is updated with the new description while car-
rying over compatible keyword/source evidence.
Keep_both (default). This default is applied
when entity or semantic similarity is low, or when
no candidate satisﬁes the merge or replacement
conditions. It preserves potentially novel rela-
tions when aggregation or supersession is not suf-
ﬁciently supported. Appendices A and B provide
threshold values and concrete decision-log exam-
ples.

4.4 Consistency: Staged Update and Commit
All updates follow a staged commit protocol: de-
cisions ﬁrst produce upsert and removal lists, U
and R, from the current auxiliary state; the main
hypergraph is then updated; and the auxiliary LSH
state is committed only after successful write-back.
If the main-graph write fails, the auxiliary state
remains unchanged and the stage can be retried.
This protocol prevents the auxiliary index from ad-
vancing beyond the persisted hypergraph state. Af-
ter candidate retrieval, the pairwise scoring and de-
cision cost per batch is O(|∆E low
t | · M ), where
M is the candidate cap. This does not remove
extraction cost for newly arrived documents, but
avoids detailed comparison against all historical
low-order edges and avoids re-extracting the his-
torical corpus.
5 Experiments and Analysis
Our experiments evaluate HyperRAG-IHM from
four complementary perspectives. First, we test
whether incremental maintenance preserves an-
swer quality against full reconstruction. Second,
we measure whether this quality is achieved with
lower update cost. Third, we examine whether the
proposed actions maintain the relationship layer
rather than merely appending new edges. Finally,
we evaluate whether the effect generalizes across
datasets, answer models, and ablation settings.
5.1 Experimental Setup
We summarize the shared experimental context in
this subsection, while task-speciﬁc question splits
and reporting details are introduced in the corre-
sponding experiment subsections.
Datasets. We evaluate the proposed method on
four datasets with different scale and domain char-
acteristics. Pathology contains 5,297 medical
pathology documents and 5,289 indexed chunks,
and serves as the primary dataset for the detailed
quality, robustness, maintenance, ablation, and
efﬁciency analyses. Neurology contains 12,370
medical neurology documents and 12,370 indexed
chunks, providing a larger medical-domain gener-
alization setting. MixCorp contains 687 mixed-
domain input documents and 817 indexed chunks
derived from heterogeneous QA sources, provid-
ing an additional cross-domain setting. MuSiQue
(Trivedi et al., 2022) is evaluated using a difﬁculty-
stratiﬁed 40% subset containing 11,960 Wikipedia
passages and 150 multi-hop questions, providing a
larger open-domain generalization setting.
Compared systems. The main method is
HyperRAG-IHM (HyperRAG with Incremental
Hyperedge Maintenance ), which updates an
existing HyperRAG index by processing newly
arrived texts and their extracted hyperedges. The
primary reference is HyperRAG full recon-
struction, which rebuilds the HyperRAG index
from the expanded corpus and is used to assess
whether incremental maintenance preserves
full-reconstruction quality. For broader context on
the primary dataset, we also include GraphRAG
(Edge et al. , 2024), LightRAG (Guo et al. , 2025),
and an embedding-only chunk-retrieval RAG
baseline ( Lewis et al. , 2020; Karpukhin et al. ,
2020). Their results serve as reference points on
the primary dataset.
Evaluation protocol. We adopt the evaluation
protocol used by HyperRAG ( Feng et al. , 2026).
Speciﬁcally, its 5D scoring-based evaluation ex-
tends the GraphRAG evaluation paradigm based
on LLM judges ( Edge et al. , 2024; Zheng
et al. , 2023; Liu et al. , 2023) to measure ab-
solute answer quality across Comprehensiveness,
Diversity, Empowerment, Logical, and Read-
ability. Its selection-based evaluation com-
pares two answers across eight dimensions—
Comprehensiveness, Empowerment, Accuracy,
Relevance, Coherence, Clarity, Logical, and Flex-
ibility—and records which answer is judged bet-
ter in each dimension. In this work, 5D scor-
ing is used for broad absolute-quality comparisons,
whereas 8D pairwise selection is used for ﬁne-
grained relative comparison: by comparing two
answers under the same judge prompt, it better
exposes relative answer-quality differences and re-
duces perturbations from score-scale calibration
and answer style. For 8D selection, we report
clear-preference rates.
Model settings. All HyperRAG index construc-
tion and hyperedge extraction use Doubao-1.5-
Lite as the construction LLM. Doubao-1.5-Lite
serves as the primary judge for the reported eval-
uations. To assess judge sensitivity, we addi-
tionally evaluate the Pathology comparison with
DeepSeek-V3.2, GLM-5, and Kimi-K2.5. For
answer generation, the main experiments use
Doubao-1.5-Lite, and cross-answer-model anal-
yses additionally use MiniMax-M2.7, Kimi-2.5,

5.2 Primary-Dataset Overall Comparison
Pathology serves as the primary dataset, with
dense medical entities, specialized terminology,
and clinically grounded relations that require fac-
tual grounding and evidence integration. We eval-
uate single-hop questions for ordinary factual re-
trieval and multi-hop questions requiring joint evi-
dence across texts. Across three repeated trials, we
sample 100 single-hop and 50 multi-hop questions
per trial; Figures 2a and 2b report the 5D averages,
with detailed scores in Appendix C.
HyperRAG-IHM achieves the highest 5D aver-
age score in both single-hop (57.80) and multi-
hop (66.77) settings, with gains mainly in Compre-
hensiveness, Logical, and Readability. We further
compare it with the HyperRAG full-reconstruction
reference using the full 8D pairwise preference
evaluation.
Figure 3 shows that HyperRAG-IHM is pre-
ferred in 58.25% of clear-preference compar-
isons, with the strongest advantages in Com-
prehensiveness (72.4%), Accuracy (64.9%), and
Empowerment (64.8%). These results indicate
that HyperRAG-IHM preserves competitive full-
reconstruction quality while showing advantages
in several coverage-oriented dimensions under the
primary judge.

5.5 Ablation Study
To isolate the proposed maintenance mechanism,

normalized entity keys. HyperRAG-IHM fur-

multi-dimensional comparison, and explicit main-

that active hyperedge maintenance is especially

6 Conclusion
We have proposed the ﬁrst incremental low-order
hyperedge maintenance method for hypergraph-
based RAG, enabling knowledge bases to absorb
new documents without full-graph reconstruction.
The core mechanism combines LSH candidate re-
trieval, multi-dimensional scoring, and a three-
action decision policy, preserving answer quality
competitive with full reconstruction under LLM-
judge evaluation. Cross-dataset, cross-answer-
model, and cross-judge evaluations support the ro-
bustness of these ﬁndings, and the efﬁciency analy-
sis conﬁrms that incremental maintenance substan-
tially reduces construction cost. Beyond Hyper-
RAG, the maintenance framework could in prin-
ciple be adapted to other structured RAG systems
that maintain entity-level relational records, pro-
viding a foundation for scalable knowledge main-
tenance under corpus growth. Future directions in-
clude learning decision thresholds from data rather
than hand-tuning them, extending three-action se-
mantic maintenance to high-order thematic hyper-
edges, and evaluating on corpora with document
revisions and temporal conﬂicts.
Limitations
The decision thresholds are empirically speciﬁed
rather than optimized; the results demonstrate ef-
fectiveness under the present settings but do not
prove optimality. Although the experiments sim-
ulate corpus expansion through incremental up-
dates, they use static datasets; true dynamic cor-
pora with document revisions, temporal conﬂicts,
and freshness-sensitive queries remain beyond the
current evaluation. Cross-judge evaluation re-
duces dependence on a single automatic evaluator,
but the current study does not include human an-
notation.
